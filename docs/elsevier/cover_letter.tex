We are pleased to submit our original research manuscript titled \textbf{''Thermal-Crosstalk-Aware Scheduling and Model Predictive Dynamic Voltage and Frequency Scaling for Spaceborne Multi-Chip AI Clusters in Deep Vacuum''} for consideration for publication in \textit{Control Engineering Practice}.

\textbf{Motivation and Relevance to \textit{Control Engineering Practice}:} \\
Modern Earth Observation satellites increasingly embed commercial off-the-shelf (COTS) multi-core neural accelerators to perform real-time autonomous data triaging in Low Earth Orbit (LEO). In the deep vacuum of space, convective heat transfer is absent ($h \approx 0$), restricting thermal dissipation strictly to solid conduction and highly non-linear fourth-order Stefan-Boltzmann radiation to deep space ($q \propto T^4$). In compact multi-chip architectures, intensive AI workloads induce severe lateral conductive heat diffusion (\textit{thermal crosstalk}), causing localized hot spots and steep spatial temperature gradients that severely degrade conventional terrestrial reactive governors (e.g., Linux ondemand and uncoordinated PID loops).

This article addresses this critical engineering control challenge by developing and evaluating \textbf{TCAS-DVFS}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Practical Control Design:} An Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) using Joseph-stabilized covariance updates estimates silicon junction temperatures under realistic sensor noise ($\sigma_s = 0.25\text{ K}$).
    \item \textbf{Constrained LTV MPC-DVFS Formulation:} Operating frequencies are coordinated at 0.5~Hz via a discrete linear time-varying Model Predictive Controller that solves a condensed convex quadratic program using a warm-started ADMM splitting iteration, enforcing a conservative 83~$^\circ$C limit and actively penalizing inter-die gradients.
    \item \textbf{Orbital Power Budgeting:} A predictive moving-average surrogate explicitly incorporates prospective direct solar flux, Earth albedo, terrestrial infrared, and eclipse transitions.
    \item \textbf{Extensive Methodological Verification:} The closed-loop control system is rigorously evaluated over a three-orbit co-simulation (17,004~s, 34,009 steps), achieving zero observed violations of the 85~$^\circ$C flight limit, dampening peak gradients by 77.6\%, and maintaining competitive inference throughput (124.38~FPS). In addition, a 30-sample Latin Hypercube Sampling (LHS) campaign ($\pm20\%$ parameter variation) provides exact Clopper-Pearson coverage bounds (11.57\%), accompanied by temporal integrator convergence tests and ASTM E1049-85 rainflow validation.
\end{enumerate}
